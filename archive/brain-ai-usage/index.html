What AI does to our brain

In the pursuit of choosing the path of least resistance we have degenerated many abilities that were once crucial for our daily life.

While generally deemed beneficial technological advancements impose a silent risk of degradation of skills that come with stronger adaption and normalized use within society. Experience shows that typically society is faster to incorporate a new technology than adapting towards its responsible use and proper adaption.

While there are a multitude of risks such as:

  • The risk of overreliance
  • Risk of improper alignment

I will particularly focus on the risk of mental deterioration.

Cognitive debt as a societal problem

As definied before overreliance on certain technologies leads us to incur cognitive debt. For the longest time we didnt consider or simply didnt mind the lingering side effects of automation (International Civil Aviation Organization, 1992) as it has little to no impact on our daily life, due to constant availability of its replacement.

Although this shift is acceptable in many cases, warning signs start to appear that the disruption by AI will be a bigger and fundamentally different one. Impacting our critical thinking which is considerably less negligible than skills such as complex navigation.

As AI makes handing off complex thinking easy and convenient. The danger becomes that we slowly erode parts of our ability to critically evaluate

Mechanisms of Cognitive debt

There has been a general change in communication, due to influences such as social media, like the visible shift towards the use of emotionality in speech rather than grounding facts in science based literature or reputable systems. (Lukin et al., 2017)

While the current state of research

As expected current research shows that cognitive engagement is significantly lowered when largely offloading tasks to LLMs or AI Tools (Kosmyna et al., 2025).

The processes of neural plasticity are complex, however following a notion of treating the brain like a muscle that can atrophy given little stimulation seems to be a good intuition for responsible AI use.

Keeping your mind active and broadly speaking “training” improves crystallized intelligence and cognitive reserve which has shown to delay effects of early dementia due to compensation via other neurons. Logically extrapolating that principle leads us to the conclusion that handing-off more and more tasks to AI reduces cognitive effort therefore inducing weaker long-term retention and mental deconditioning in severe overuse.

I consider myself an avid user of AI so reading into the literature as a Software Engineer was little surprising given the noticable effects of starting to forget basic syntax when overusing AI for a prolonged period of time.

How to avoid cognitive debt

is not to avoid AI but to use it consciously in its ability to quickly execute or brainstorm with you not for you.
